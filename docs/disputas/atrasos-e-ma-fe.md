# Dispute delays and suspected bad faith

**Scenario:** a counterparty stops responding, repeatedly postpones a required step, or claims a dispute is resolved without a verifiable record in the authorized application.

## Warning signs
- Requests to wait indefinitely without a clear reason or an updated status in the actual trade workflow.
- Pressure to release crypto or send an additional payment to end a delay.
- Contradictory messages about payment, delivery, or a purported arbitration decision.
- Requests to abandon the documented dispute channel for private messages.

A slow response alone is not proof of fraud. Network conditions, provider reviews, time zones, and legitimate misunderstandings can also cause delays.

## What to do
1. Check the current trade and dispute status using the application's authenticated interface.
2. Preserve the agreed terms, original messages, transaction identifiers, timestamps, and any status changes.
3. Distinguish what you verified independently from what the other party merely claims.
4. Use the support or dispute process actually offered by the application. Check its published deadlines and evidence requirements rather than assuming a universal timeline.
5. Do not release escrowed assets, repeat a payment, or change the recipient merely to make a delay disappear.

## If the delay continues
Keep a concise chronology of attempts to resolve the issue and any responses. Share only relevant records with the authorized reviewer. Do not publish identities, complete account details, or private evidence on social media.

## Limits
Not every application provides arbitration, escalation, appeals, or fixed response deadlines. This guide does not establish a right to a refund, a penalty, or a specific ruling. It does not claim that Sails Protocol automatically detects bad-faith delays.

**Claim status:** general educational guidance, not an implemented dispute policy.
