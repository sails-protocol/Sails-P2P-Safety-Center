# Mule accounts and compromised payment details

**Scenario:** payment details belong to an unexpected person or an account that may be controlled by someone other than the apparent trader.

## Warning signs
- A sudden change in the recipient, destination, or details of a payment method previously registered or agreed for the trade.
- An explanation that a friend, employee, or unrelated business must receive or refund the funds.
- A previously agreed payment destination is replaced immediately before settlement.

## What to do
1. Compare the actual payment recipient with the agreed instructions before sending funds.
2. Verify changes through the application's authorized trade channel, not a forwarded screenshot or external chat.
3. Stop and use the applicable support or dispute process if ownership or instructions remain unclear.
4. Preserve only necessary records and avoid exposing personal or banking data publicly.

## Limits
A third-party or business account is not automatically fraudulent. A long account history, account signature, or trader reputation cannot conclusively prove who controls an account now. Payment reversals and bank-side investigations remain outside crypto escrow.

**Claim status:** general safety guidance. Payment-account trust ramping is described in RFC-021, but its presence does not guarantee protection from mule accounts or account takeover.
