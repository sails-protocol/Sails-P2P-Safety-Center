# Payments involving third parties

**Scenario:** someone asks you to pay a different account than agreed, or payment arrives from someone other than the buyer.

## Warning signs
- An unexpected change to the beneficiary, payment destination, or payment details of any method previously registered or agreed for the trade.
- Pressure to pay quickly or continue outside the agreed trade flow.
- A request to refund to a destination different from the original payment source or outside the agreed payment method.

## What to do
1. Do not treat a name or identifier displayed by a bank, payment provider, or wallet as sufficient proof of safety.
2. Compare the beneficiary, destination, and payment-method details with those previously registered or formally agreed in the trade.
3. If details differ, **stop the payment** and use the applicable support or dispute channel.
4. Preserve payment records and relevant messages without publicly sharing personal or financial information.

Joint accounts, business accounts, and third-party payments can have legitimate uses. A mismatch requires verification, not an automatic fraud accusation.

**Status:** general guidance; does not claim the protocol verifies ownership of accounts, wallets, or other payment destinations.
